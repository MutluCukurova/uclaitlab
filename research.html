Enhancing Teacher Interventions by Designing an Adaptive Support for Online Collaborative Problem Solving with Large Language Models

Contact: Kester Wong

Research summary

Collaborative Problem Solving (CPS) is a multilevel process involving the perspectives of both individuals and groups, in which activated cognitive domain knowledge and the social aspects of interaction are dynamically engaged towards shared understanding (Luckin et al., 2017). Existing studies have explored the use of coaching (D’Mello et al., 2024) and social robots (Nasir et al., 2023) to support and improve aspects of CPS competency; however, just-in-time guidance on CPS processes and the alignment of such support to teaching practice remain underexplored. This research proposes an adaptive support for CPS involving scaffolding of problem-solving and collaborative processes, taking a data-driven approach to the design of an adaptive scaffold intervention. Explorations investigate the use of transformer-based models for CPS diagnosis, with these diagnoses and interventions subsequently compared to those produced by teachers as a preliminary step towards designing an adaptive scaffold intervention system that helps teachers better support students in CPS.

Supporting Students' Regulation of Learning in Small Group Collaboration in Higher Education: A Multimodal Learning Analytics Approach

Contact: Wannapon Suraworachet

Research summary

Effective collaboration requires awareness of group challenges and timely regulation. This research investigates how learning analytics can detect challenge moments in small-group discourse and support students' regulation through an NLP-based predictive model and a feedback dashboard. Findings from a real-world deployment show that analytics-driven feedback can raise students' awareness, particularly at the individual level, though group regulation remains limited with practical challenges. This work offers a theoretically grounded, empirically validated framework for detecting and supporting socially shared regulation of learning in authentic higher education settings.

Keywords

Supporting SRL in Online Learning through LA-Driven LLM Feedback: Examining Learner Perceptions

Contact: Mutluhan Yilmaz

Research summary

This doctoral research investigates the role of LLM-generated, trace-based feedback in supporting self-regulated learning (SRL) skills in online higher education. Drawing on learning analytics data, it compares LLM- and tutor-generated feedback across multiple feedback instances over a term, moving beyond single-session designs to examine longitudinal SRL development. Both the quality of the feedback produced and learners’ perceptions of it are examined, contextualising its potential instructional value. Findings are expected to contribute to the design of scalable, high-quality feedback interventions in online learning contexts.

Keywords

  • feedback

Benchmarking and Mitigating Gender Bias Through Scaffolding Interaction in Educational Applications of LLMs

Contact: Yishan Du

Research summary

Large language models are rapidly entering educational workflows, including teacher-facing tasks such as formative feedback and assessment, where implicit and context-dependent gender bias may surface through subtle shifts in tone, emphasis, and pedagogical framing with cumulative downstream consequences for students’ motivation, academic identity, and long-term trajectories. Two gaps remain in the field: a lack of robust, replicable, and interpretable benchmarking methods that detect subtle bias beyond template-based audits; and limited empirical evidence on how teacher–LLM interaction co-constructs, amplifies, or corrects model tendencies. This work therefore advances a two-strand programme connecting technical bias benchmarking with interactional bias co-construction. The first strand develops an education-grounded auditing framework using counterfactual educational inputs that differ only in gender cues, benchmarking representative LLMs on authentic student-writing feedback tasks via embedding-space mapping while controlling for stochasticity, with complementary qualitative and text-analytic interpretation. The second strand brings teachers into the loop through a workshop-style behavioural study under no-AI versus AI-assisted conditions, capturing decision changes, confidence shifts, adoption/editing behaviour, and bias indices, towards a taxonomy of human–AI bias interaction regimes.

Keywords

  • gender bias
  • large language models
  • educational fairness
  • counterfactual auditing
  • teacher–AI interaction
  • formative feedback
  • bias benchmarking
